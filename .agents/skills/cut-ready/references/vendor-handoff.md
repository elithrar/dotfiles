# Producer interpretation and physical proof

## Verify current requirements

Check the exact producer, service, material, thickness, and finish when those rules affect the current task. Do not refresh unrelated process decisions for a narrow cosmetic revision. Retrieve accepted formats, feature limits, tolerances, operation assignments, and configuration rules from current official sources rather than copying old numerical snapshots.

Useful starting points, not universal specifications:

- [SendCutSend general guidelines](https://sendcutsend.com/guidelines/), [sheet cutting](https://sendcutsend.com/guidelines/sheet-cutting/), and [CNC machining](https://sendcutsend.com/guidelines/cnc-machining/).
- [Protolabs machining toolkit](https://www.protolabs.com/resources/design-for-machining-toolkit/) and [thread representation](https://www.protolabs.com/en-gb/resources/design-tips/threading-considerations-for-cnc-machining/).
- [Roland print/cut paths](https://downloadcenter.rolanddg.com/contents/manuals/VW6_English/ffk1725848718165.html), [StickerApp cutlines](https://stickerapp.com/support/artwork-design/templates-guidelines/how-to-make-your-own-cutlines), and the chosen print product's artwork requirements.

Use the producer's accepted channel to communicate requirements absent from geometry: tolerances/inspection, thread services, finishing, masking, secondary operations, and surface expectations. Identify who performs support removal, cure, tapping, insert installation, deburring, coating, or other necessary steps. Distinguish a visual material shader from a viable physical finish.

## Inspect the actual preview/configuration

Upload only when authorized. Confirm interpreted units/size, part count, critical features, process, material, stock thickness, quantity, finish, and secondary services against the checked file. For bends, verify detection, angle/direction, stationary face, and orientation. For artwork, verify final cut behavior, bleed, margins, borders, white/transparency, and proof-only versus production paths.

Investigate auto-repairs, missing features, unexpected classification, warnings, or price anomalies that may indicate misinterpretation. Do not correct a preview by arbitrary scaling. Record the reviewed configuration and exact file revision; successful upload alone is not evidence of correct design intent.

## Match physical proof to the uncertainty

Use a 1:1 paper/cardboard outline for size or mounting placement; it cannot establish 3D engagement, snap action, strength, or thread fit. Use an appropriate coupon, prototype, or first article when unresolved fit, process variation, or failure consequence warrants it. Check the actual assembly, postprocess state, or physical color/finish as relevant. Reuse valid proof for unchanged features rather than requiring a new prototype for every cosmetic edit.

Report digital interpretation and physical evidence separately. A passed preview, slice, or quote cannot validate strength, sealing, thermal behavior, finish, or regulatory suitability. Do not place an order, approve production/proofs, purchase material, or send machine commands without the required authorization.
