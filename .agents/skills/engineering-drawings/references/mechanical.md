# Parts, housings, and 2D views of 3D models

## Establish geometry and orientation

Identify the authoritative model/part revision, units, material, process, intended mating interfaces, and finished-state requirements. Preserve accepted geometry on drawing-only changes. Separate known dimensions from photo estimates or hidden inferred geometry.

Derive views from that model where available. Declare first- or third-angle projection consistently; align orthographic views and use sections/details to reveal hidden features. Use an auxiliary view for true shape when needed. An isometric/perspective view can orient the reader but must not substitute for measurable geometry. Do not infer exact dimensions from a pretty render.

Check each view against the actual model: silhouette, handedness, axes, hole/slot centers, wall/floor thickness, recesses, bosses, opening planes, and relationships between features. Verify section cutting plane, direction, labels, and material hatching. Ensure hidden lines and center marks describe real geometry. Confirm the views describe the same part, not independently plausible sketches.

## Define what must be made and inspected

- Choose functional datums/references and locate critical features from them. Dimension enough to manufacture and inspect without scaling the sheet. Avoid conflicting duplicate dimensions, closed chains, and unintentional tolerance stacks; mark noncontrolling reference dimensions as such.
- Specify required sizes, positions, quantities, depths, through/blind status, radii/chamfers, and relevant fits/tolerances. Separate nominal geometry from permitted variation. Do not invent a blanket tolerance from decimal places or use GD&T symbols without a defined, inspectable requirement and applicable convention.
- For threads, specify size/pitch or series, internal/external, class and hand when applicable, and usable engagement. Distinguish thread depth from drill depth/runout. Verify clearance, screw bottom-out, wall/floor, and assembly/tool access. Match the selected manufacturing route; a cosmetic thread or circular hole alone is not a thread specification.
- State material/condition, finish and treated surfaces where needed. Identify whether fit-critical dimensions apply before or after finishing. Include required edge treatment without rounding intentional sharp features by default.
- For housings, check installed envelopes, mounting/retention, lid travel, connectors beneath panels, fasteners, wire bends, glands, tool access, and service removal. External box dimensions do not prove usable internal space. A diagrammatic enclosure boundary is not a validated packing or drilling layout.
- Reconcile assembly balloons, quantities, hardware, and purchased-part identities with the BOM. Distinguish assembled arrangement, individual part definition, and installation interfaces.

## Audit the final projection

Measure critical exported geometry and callouts independently against the model/specification; do not accept annotation values as measurements. Verify page and view scales after transforms, unit conversions, and export. For a broken/diagrammatic view, verify the labeled true dimensions and break convention instead of measuring the gap.

Inspect every dimension's witness lines, arrowheads, leader target, symbol, precision and tolerance. Use sections to verify hidden floors, blind depths, and clearances; a bounding box or attractive shaded view cannot establish them. Recheck all dependent views after a model change.

Keep digital agreement separate from manufacturing feasibility and physical proof. Do not call fit, strength, sealing, or process capability validated without the appropriate evidence.
