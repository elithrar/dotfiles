# Standards and evidence boundary

Select the convention required by the project; do not mix incompatible notation or claim compliance from appearance. Retrieve the applicable edition and relevant primary requirements when needed. Public catalogue descriptions establish scope, not access to every normative clause. If only those descriptions are available, say the drawing uses established conventions, not that a full compliance audit passed.

Primary reference anchors checked 14 October 2026:

- [IEC 61082-1:2014](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/4469): electrotechnical document, diagram, drawing, and table presentation.
- [IEC 60617 database](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/2723): graphical symbols for electrical diagrams.
- [ASME Y14.100-2017](https://www.asme.org/codes-standards/find-codes-standards/y14-100-engineering-drawing-practices): engineering drawings and associated lists.
- [ASME Y14.3-2012 (R2024)](https://www.asme.org/codes-standards/find-codes-standards/y14-3-orthographic-pictorial-views) and [Y14.5-2018 (R2024)](https://www.asme.org/codes-standards/find-codes-standards/y14-5-dimensioning-tolerancing): views, dimensioning, and tolerancing.
- [ISO 128-2:2022](https://www.iso.org/standard/83355.html): technical line and leader conventions; use the relevant ISO projection/dimensioning standards when the project specifies ISO.

For actual hardware and manufacturing limits, prioritize the exact manufacturer's current drawing/application specification and applicable producer requirements. Keep source provenance available off-sheet. Do not invent firm-specific standards, reproduce protected standard sheets, fabricate approvals, or imply professional certification.
